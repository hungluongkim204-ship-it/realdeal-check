Price History Tools: How to Check What Something Really Cost

Every real deal verdict starts with the same question: what did this item actually sell for over the past three months?

These free tools answer it. Here’s what each one shows, how to use it, and where each stops being useful.

CamelCamelCamel (Amazon, US/UK)

What it shows: the complete price history of products sold on Amazon — every price change over time, plotted as a chart, for both Amazon’s own price and third-party sellers. You can see the all-time low, the 90-day trend, and exactly when prices moved.

How to use it:

  1. Copy the Amazon product URL or the ASIN.
  2. Paste it into camelcamelcamel.com and search.
  3. Read the chart: note the lowest price in the last 90 days and the current price.
  4. Optionally set a price alert below a target.

Limits: it works only for Amazon marketplaces. It won’t capture in-store-only promotions or coupons that require specific payment methods. Very new products may have little or no history, which is itself a warning sign.

Keepa (Amazon, deeper data)

What it shows: the same Amazon price history as CamelCamelCamel, presented as an overlay directly on the Amazon product page, plus buy-box history, third-party new/used prices, and stock movements.

How to use it:

  1. Install the Keepa browser extension, then open an Amazon product page.
  2. Hover over the chart to read the exact price on any past date.
  3. Compare the current price against the 90-day low.

Limits: it is Amazon only. The free tier covers the price history most shoppers need; some advanced data is paid.

Google Shopping price insights

What it shows: for many products listed on Google Shopping, a price history graph and “lowest price” indicators appear in the product panel.

  1. Search for the product on Google and open Shopping results.
  2. Select a product and look for price history or price trends.
  3. Compare the current offer against the recent low across retailers.

Limits: coverage is inconsistent, history depth varies, and Google doesn’t document exactly which merchants are included. Treat it as a quick first check and cross-retailer sanity check.

PriceSpy / PriceRunner (UK and Europe)

What they show: multi-retailer price comparison with price history for products sold across UK and European shops. Enter a product, see which retailers currently have it cheapest, and view how the price has moved over time.

  1. Search the product by name or barcode on pricespy.co.uk.
  2. Open the product page and check the price history chart for the last 90 days.
  3. Compare the current best offer against that low, including delivery cost.

Limits: coverage depends on which retailers feed the service. History depth varies by product.

idealo (Germany)

What it shows: Germany’s largest price comparison portal — current offers from German retailers plus price history for many products.

  1. Search the product on idealo.de.
  2. Open the product page and look for the Preisverlauf chart.
  3. Read the lowest price of the last 90 days and compare with the current best offer.

Limits: it is German-market oriented, and coverage depends on participating retailers.

The limits that apply to all of them

  • No tool sees everything: in-store prices, app-exclusive deals, and member pricing are generally invisible.
  • Coupons and checkout discounts may not appear in the tracked price. Compare the final checkout price against the 90-day low.
  • History gaps happen: a product with no chart isn’t proof of a scam, but the percentage-off claim is unverified.
  • Refurbished and used units have their own price tracks. Compare like with like.

Pick whichever tool covers the shop you’re buying from, find the 90-day low, and you have the one number that matters.
