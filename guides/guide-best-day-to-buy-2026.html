The Best Day to Buy in the 2026 Holiday Season, Category by Category

Holiday sales are not one event — they’re a sequence, and different product categories hit their deepest discounts at different points in it.

Adobe’s 2026 US holiday forecast (based on over a trillion retail site visits) projects which days each category bottoms out. Here’s the calendar, and how to shop it.

The dates that matter

  • Thanksgiving — Thursday, November 26, 2026
  • Black Friday — Friday, November 27, 2026 (the day after US Thanksgiving; widely adopted in the UK too)
  • Cyber Monday — Monday, November 30, 2026
  • Green Monday — Monday, December 14, 2026 (the second Monday in December, historically one of December’s biggest online shopping days)
  • Super Saturday — Saturday, December 19, 2026 (the last Saturday before Christmas)
  • Boxing Day — Saturday, December 26, 2026 (the UK’s traditional clearance kickoff)
  • January sales — early to mid-January

Electronics and computers → Cyber Monday (Nov 30)

Adobe’s 2026 forecast expects the deepest discounts on electronics (headphones, speakers, gadgets) and laptops/computers on Cyber Monday, with peak discounts of roughly 30% off listed price at the season’s lowest point. If you’re shopping for gadgets, there is little reason to buy on Black Friday itself — the category is projected to bottom out three days later. Do check the 90-day low before you commit: a “Cyber Monday deal” is only worth it if it actually beats the past three months of pricing.

TVs → Black Friday (Nov 27)

TVs are projected to hit their peak discounts (around 23% off) on Black Friday. This is one of the most competitive categories, and doorbuster-style pricing on big screens is concentrated on the day itself. Unlike electronics generally, waiting for Cyber Monday is not expected to improve TV prices.

Toys and games → Black Friday (Nov 27)

Toys are projected to see peak discounts of roughly 29% on Black Friday — and popular items genuinely sell out, unlike many categories where stock carries through to Cyber Monday. If a specific toy is on your list, buy when it hits its 90-day low rather than waiting for a deeper cut that may never come.

Clothing and shoes → Black Friday (Nov 27)

Apparel peaks at around 23% off on Black Friday in Adobe’s forecast. Sizes are the constraint here — the risk isn’t the price rising, it’s your size disappearing.

Appliances and furniture → Black Friday (Nov 27)

Adobe’s category data projects peak discounts of roughly 18% for appliances (vacuums, kitchen) and furniture on Black Friday. These are among the categories where site-level estimates are used where no published 2026 figure exists, so treat the size of the discount as approximate and the timing guidance as the reliable part.

Sporting goods → Thanksgiving (Nov 26)

Sporting goods and fitness equipment are projected to peak earliest — around Thanksgiving itself (Nov 26), at roughly 19% off. If you’re shopping for a treadmill, weights, or cycling gear, the deals start before Black Friday does.

Green Monday (Dec 14) → last call for online orders

Green Monday isn’t projected to beat Black Friday or Cyber Monday on discounts. Its significance is logistics: it is near the final window for pre-Christmas delivery. After this week, check each retailer’s stated delivery cut-off before counting on a gift arriving in time.

Boxing Day and January sales → UK clearance season

For UK shoppers the season doesn’t end at Christmas. Boxing Day (Dec 26) opens the traditional clearance period, and January sales run through roughly the first two weeks of the month. Clearance prices can be excellent — but clearance isn’t automatically a bargain. Compare against the 90-day low.

One rule that outranks the calendar

Timing tells you when discounts tend to be deepest for a category. The 90-day low tells you whether this specific price is actually good. Check the calendar for your category — then verify the price history for your product before you buy.
